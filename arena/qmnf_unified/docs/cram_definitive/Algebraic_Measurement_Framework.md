## Mathlib and Coq already formalize the algebraic skeleton

The CRT ring isomorphism that undergirds the entire QMNF architecture is thoroughly formalized across multiple proof assistants. In **Lean 4/Mathlib**, `ZMod.chineseRemainder` establishes the ring isomorphism `ZMod (m * n) ≃+* ZMod m × ZMod n` for coprime m, n. The general ideal-theoretic version `Ideal.quotientInfRingEquivPiQuotient` proves that for pairwise coprime ideals I₁, …, Iₖ in a commutative ring R, the quotient R/(⋂Iᵢ) is ring-isomorphic to the product ∏R/Iᵢ—exactly the abstract algebraic backbone of QMNF's multi-lane architecture. [Florisvandoorn](https://florisvandoorn.com/LeanCourse24/docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/QuotientOperations.html) Euler's totient function is fully formalized [Lean Community](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib-overview.html) (`Nat.totient`) with the divisor sum formula, multiplicativity, and prime-power cases, [Lean Community](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Data/Nat/Totient.html) though the **Carmichael function λ(n) remains unformalized** in any major proof assistant. Since λ(n) = lcm of λ(pᵢ^kᵢ) factors, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmichael_function) it could be constructed from Mathlib's existing `Nat.totient` and `Nat.lcm` infrastructure, but nobody has done so yet.

In **Coq**, the most impactful formalization is **Fiat-Crypto** (MIT PLV), which generates correct-by-construction finite field arithmetic code currently deployed in BoringSSL, Chrome, Firefox, and the Linux kernel. [IACR](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/549.pdf) Fiat-Crypto uses Montgomery and Solinas representations rather than CRT-based multi-precision arithmetic, leaving the specific RNS/CRT representation strategy used by QMNF **unverified in any production proof assistant**. The Mathematical Components (MathComp/SSReflect) library provides extensive finite group and ring infrastructure from the Feit-Thompson proof, while da Silva et al. (2021) formalized the full ring-theoretic CRT in PVS with three isomorphism theorems.

Critically for QMNF's idempotent-projector theory, Mathlib's `RingTheory.Idempotents` module formalizes **complete orthogonal idempotents** and proves `CompleteOrthogonalIdempotents.bijective_pi`—the theorem that a family of complete orthogonal idempotents {eᵢ} induces a ring isomorphism R ≃+* ∏ R/⟨1 − eᵢ⟩. [Lean Community](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Idempotents.html) This is precisely the CRT decomposition viewed through the projector lens. The lifting theorem `CompleteOrthogonalIdempotents.lift_of_isNilpotent_ker` proves that idempotents lift through ring maps whose kernels consist of nilpotent elements— [Lean Community](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Idempotents.html) a Hensel-type result directly connecting nilpotent elements to the stability of CRT decompositions under perturbation.

## Phase transitions, nilpotents, and the measurement tier system

The QMNF framework exhibits a **sharp phase transition** in its CRT torus coverage data. Empirical testing at N = 500,000 shows all 10 pairs and all 20 triples from the basis {3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17} achieve **100% fiber saturation**. But excluding mod-3 collapses the quintuple 5 × 7 × 11 × 13 × 17 from full saturation to just **69.97% coverage**—a discrete phase transition governed by the smallest modulus. [RECOMBINANT_CRT_BRIDGE_TECHNICAL_PACKET](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1dpxoU_OStrVPTZ0L9cwym0NdtmCPvfX_W-yhZ0N5FBw/edit) This resonates with the classical **Friedgut-Kalai sharp threshold theorem** (1996) for symmetric monotone properties, though no formal connection to Boolean function analysis has been established. The DKAM boundary condition deg < ρ is itself a sharp algebraic phase transition: at deg = ρ the system enters "Tier 2 / Boundary" (marginal stability), and at deg > ρ, topological protection fails catastrophically.

**Nilpotent elements** enter the QMNF framework through the composite-as-structure paradigm. Standard RNS engineering avoids composite moduli because Z/nZ with n = p^k (k ≥ 2) contains nilpotent elements (p is nilpotent: p^k ≡ 0). The QMNF framework **deliberately uses composite sub-lanes** in the Recombinant CRT Bridge architecture, with moduli of the form p^e drawn from the Safe Prime Basis {2, 3, 5, 7, 11}. [RECOMBINANT_CRT_BRIDGE_TECHNICAL_PACKET](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1dpxoU_OStrVPTZ0L9cwym0NdtmCPvfX_W-yhZ0N5FBw/edit) The internal survey confirms this is unprecedented: "No work deliberately exploits the internal factorization of composite moduli as beneficial structural elements." [compass_artifact_wf-af4807ed-7b5c-4409-b19a-97c27ee25f54_text_markdown.md](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1w32ykNITKrL02HN16gtOx3JkwCO7MksTi2KL2Ja_huA/edit) Mathlib's `existsUnique_isIdempotentElem_eq_of_ker_isNilpotent` theorem—unique lifting of idempotents through maps with nilpotent kernels—provides the formal foundation for understanding how the CRT decomposition remains stable even when individual lanes contain nilpotent elements.

The **Carmichael function and measurement depth** connection maps naturally to the QMNF scaffold depth concept. The Carmichael function λ(n) determines the exponent of (Z/nZ)×—the maximum multiplicative order of any unit. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmichael_function) In QMNF's iterative scaffold extension (adding primes to the CRT basis), the "measurement depth" at scaffold level k corresponds to how many K-Elimination winding numbers can be extracted before the anchor modulus is exhausted. The iterated Carmichael function (applying λ repeatedly) studied by Belhaouari et al. (2021) provides a natural notion of "depth of reduction" that parallels QMNF's scaffold depth, though this connection remains informal.

## Conclusion: a framework rich in novel structure, early in formal verification

The QMNF ecosystem occupies a unique position: its algebraic foundations (CRT isomorphisms, F_p² arithmetic, idempotent decompositions) are thoroughly formalized in Lean 4/Mathlib and Coq/MathComp, but its **novel constructions—K-Elimination, the PSPI functor, phase-locked residue lanes, the DKAM persistence theorem, and the entanglement-measurement hierarchy—have no external prior art** and only partial internal Lean 4 formalization. The `t_infinite_dkam_ns` theorem stands as the strongest formally verified result, proving global DKAM persistence for all valid NS scaffolds with zero sorry. [QmnfNS_Extended.lean](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1145jRpGB31MVdnNQB1XaTj6dFBEOyuDj5oAAP7QEEAo/edit) The PSPI morphism Φ(2.2) is proved by definitional equality. [QmnfNS_Extended.lean](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1145jRpGB31MVdnNQB1XaTj6dFBEOyuDj5oAAP7QEEAo/edit) But the bridge from discrete to continuum (OP-2), the full functoriality of Φ across all 7 theorem correspondences, and the Carmichael-depth connection remain open.

For researchers seeking to advance formal verification of this framework, the highest-value targets are: (1) formalizing the Carmichael function λ(n) in Mathlib (estimated effort: small, building on `Nat.totient` and `orderOf`); (2) closing the ~6 remaining `sorry` statements in the PSPI Lean 4 module, particularly `preserves_independence` which requires the Vandermonde determinant nonvanishing theorem; and (3) establishing a machine-verified connection between Mathlib's `CompleteOrthogonalIdempotents.bijective_pi` and the QMNF K-Elimination formula—a bridge that would ground K-Elimination in Mathlib's existing ring decomposition infrastructure rather than treating it as an axiomatically independent construction.
